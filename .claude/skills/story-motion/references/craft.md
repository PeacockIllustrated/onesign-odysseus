# Craft rules

Every rule here came back as a review note on a real piece at least once. Read them before the
first build and check against them before sharing each round.

## Story and timing
- **Don't cram. Add time.** A rushed beat is fixed by making the film longer, not by speeding the
  beat up. Comedy, reveals and payoffs need a held moment.
- **One continuous take.** The hero object carries the viewer between places; the camera follows it.
  No cuts, no fades.
- **If the brief says an object keeps moving, it never stops or leaves its path.** Characters move
  around it; the object doesn't wait for them.
- **Deadpan is funny.** A character being completely serious while doing something absurd (flipping
  mid-air in slow motion to read a page as it flies past) lands harder than mugging.
- **Order things as they'd really happen.** Install the backing panel before the letters; light
  the sign after the last letter is fixed; the celebration after the job is done.
- **Night falls gradually.** Dusk should start while the work is still going on, not snap on.

## Motion
- **Nothing snaps.** A hand jumping from hip to target in one frame is the most visible flaw there
  is. Ease every pose change, including returns to rest.
- **Nothing clips.** A parcel passing through a van door, a window drawn over a person in front of
  it, a hand through a table. Check draw order and clearances in close crops.
- **Anticipation, action, follow-through, settle** on every throw, jump, landing and catch.
- **Purposeful hands.** When a character works (fixing, typing, carrying), the hands do the job:
  reach, press, tap, hold. Idle hands dangling during a task read as fake.
- **Motion blur on fast things**, camera smear on fast pans, but never on the subject the camera
  is tracking.

## Look
- **Real-world objects look real enough.** Square corners where they're square, real wheels, a
  proper hard hat (a dome with a wrapping brim, not a flat cap perched on top). A vehicle or tool that
  looks like a toy undermines everything around it.
- **Gestures read at a glance** — especially at phone size. Thumbs-up = wide fist + short stout
  thumb. Check in a close crop.
- **Decoration earns its place.** A graphic on a sign should look designed (a proper sprig: curved
  stem, veined leaves, bud, bloom), not placeholder ellipses.
- **Clear stage behind the key action.** Move busy props (bookshelves) away from hero beats.
- **Brand marks are sacred.** Filled from the real path, never redrawn, recoloured only where the
  brand allows.

## Framing
- **Phone first.** Check every beat at 9:16. The payoff shot pushes in on the face (~2×); nothing
  important sits at the frame edge (a tick badge, a thumb, the end of a panel).
- **Pull out for wide things.** A long object landing (a fascia panel) needs a wider key so both
  ends are in frame on a phone.

## Process
- **Review on the live page; render only when asked.** Video takes ~15 minutes; the live page is
  instant. Stop renders immediately when the user wants to give more notes.
- **Contact sheets for every change**, both orientations, and read them — don't assume.
- **Determinism check** on every round (the stills script reports it).
- **Keep the link stable** — update the same review page each round.
